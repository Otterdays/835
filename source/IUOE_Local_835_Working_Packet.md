## Evidence-handling rules

- Keep the original file or message intact. Make a working copy rather than editing the original.
- Record who supplied it, when it was received, and whether the source had firsthand knowledge.
- For messages, preserve the full thread, sender, recipient, date, and time; do not crop away context.
- Label estimates as estimates. Record the method used to calculate staffing, overtime, or wage impact.
- Do not collect or circulate inmate information, access codes, post orders, emergency plans, personal medical data, or other prison-security material.
- Do not secretly record a conversation without first obtaining advice about Pennsylvania law, workplace rules, and the specific setting.
- Keep legal advice and attorney communications in a separate, access-controlled file and follow counsel's instructions concerning privilege.

# 2. Exact questions and demands

## A. Questions for Local 835 leadership and the bargaining team

### Contract status and extension

1. Please provide the complete signed CBA, every appendix and wage schedule, every side letter or memorandum, and every amendment that governed this bargaining unit through July 1, 2026.
2. What exact clause sets the expiration or renewal date? Was any notice required to prevent automatic renewal, and who sent it?
3. Is August 31, 2026 an agreed extension of the CBA, a no-strike extension, a bargaining target, a mediation date, or only an informal deadline?
4. Who agreed to the August 31 arrangement, on what date, and with what authority?
5. Please provide the signed writing or all messages and bargaining notes that record the arrangement.
6. Which provisions were expressly continued: wages and benefits, grievance processing, arbitration, management rights, union security, dues checkoff, no-strike/no-lockout, seniority, staffing, and layoff rules?
7. Has either party asserted that impasse exists? If yes, identify the date, subjects, last offers, and supporting facts. Has the union disputed that assertion in writing?

### Bargaining record and proposals

8. On what dates have the parties met, who attended each session, and what next sessions are scheduled?
9. Please provide each written employer proposal, union proposal, counterproposal, package proposal, tentative agreement, and written withdrawal in chronological order.
10. Which items are tentative agreements, which remain open, and which were withdrawn? Are tentative agreements conditional on a complete package?
11. Are there bargaining ground rules limiting member communication or proposal disclosure? Please provide them and identify their legal and practical basis.
12. Has any complete tentative agreement been reached or orally accepted? If so, what conditions remain before it becomes binding?
13. Has FMCS or another mediator been involved? If so, what is the status and next date?

### Wage proposal

14. What is the exact wage proposal for each classification, step, differential, and premium?
15. Is the reported 1% a one-time increase, an annual increase, or part of a multi-year schedule?
16. What is the proposed contract duration?
17. Is the increase retroactive to July 1? If not, what date does it begin?
18. Does the proposal change health contributions, pension contributions, paid time off, overtime, shift differential, tool allowance, licensing pay, or any other item that could offset the wage increase?
19. What is the union's costing of the employer package in dollars per employee and as a percentage of total compensation?

### Layoffs, staffing, and vacancies

20. Are the five layoffs an employer proposal, a notice of decision, a contingency, or an action already implemented?
21. Which classifications, facilities, shifts, and positions are affected? Are any individuals identified?
22. What reason has the employer given: budget, City contract scope, lack of work, reorganization, subcontracting, performance, automation, or another reason?
23. Did the union request bargaining over the decision, the effects, or both? Please provide the request and employer response.
24. What layoff, seniority, bumping, transfer, recall, benefit-continuation, severance, and notice provisions apply?
25. What is the authorized and budgeted bargaining-unit complement by facility, classification, and shift?
26. How many positions are filled, vacant, posted, frozen, temporarily covered, or performed by supervisors, non-unit staff, agency workers, subcontractors, or City employees?
27. For each vacancy, when did it open, when was it posted, how many applicants were received, and why is it unfilled?
28. What overtime, holdover, call-in, missed preventive maintenance, backlog, safety incident, and service-level data show the effects of vacancies?
29. What exact staffing-limit, minimum-complement, workload, vacancy-filling, subcontracting, or bargaining-unit-work language exists in the CBA or side letters?
30. Has that language been enforced through grievances, settlements, arbitration awards, or past practice? Please provide the record.
31. Has the union demanded the U.S. Facilities-City contract provisions, change orders, staffing assumptions, service levels, penalties, invoices, and renewal information relevant to the claimed layoffs or vacancies?

### Legal review and member governance

32. Has a lawyer reviewed the CBA expiration, the August 31 arrangement, the employer proposals, the layoffs, the staffing clause, possible information requests, and any impasse claim? If yes, in what capacity?
33. If no outside or International counsel has reviewed those materials, will the Local arrange a prompt consultation with a Pennsylvania union-side labor lawyer before recommending a final agreement, impasse position, or strike vote?
34. What Local bylaw and IUOE constitutional provisions govern bargaining-team authority, member petitions, motions, special meetings, ratification, strike authorization, quorum, notice, and secret ballots?
35. Is member ratification a condition of acceptance in this bargaining relationship? Has the employer been told in writing that any tentative agreement is subject to ratification?
36. Who is eligible to vote, what notice is required, what quorum applies, and may members inspect the complete text before voting?
37. Will the Local commit to giving members the complete proposed agreement, redline, attachments, side letters, wage tables, and tentative agreements at least five calendar days before a ratification vote?
38. Will contract ratification and strike authorization, if any, be separate questions and separate votes?
39. What process will members use to submit questions and receive written answers without retaliation or loss of meeting rights?

13. Identify whether the employer claims an inability to pay, an unwillingness to pay, a loss of revenue, a reduced scope, or another basis for its wage and layoff proposals.

19. Produce responsive records on a rolling basis, identify withheld categories and reasons, and meet promptly to resolve scope or confidentiality concerns.

## D. Alternatives to test before accepting layoffs

- Fill or repost vacancies before eliminating occupied positions.
- Use attrition, voluntary transfer, voluntary separation, or temporary vacancy controls.
- Reduce outside contracting or non-unit performance of unit work where the CBA and law permit.
- Reassign employees across facilities or shifts with training and license protection.
- Use a recall guarantee, benefit bridge, severance, and priority placement if layoffs cannot be avoided.
- Tie any staffing flexibility to enforceable minimums, vacancy-posting deadlines, quarterly reporting, and a fast grievance/arbitration path.
- Seek City contract change orders or service-level adjustments that fund needed staffing.

## Staffing clause stress test

For any suspected staffing language, answer all of these:

1. Is the number a **minimum**, a **maximum**, an **authorized complement**, or only a planning target?
2. Does it apply to the entire unit or separately by facility, classification, shift, or function?
3. Does it regulate filled positions, budgeted positions, headcount, full-time equivalents, or assignments per shift?
4. Does it permit vacancies, attrition, temporary reductions, emergencies, lack of qualified applicants, or City-directed scope changes?
5. Does the employer have a posting or hiring deadline?
6. Can subcontractors, supervisors, City employees, or temporary workers perform the work?
7. Is there a notice-and-bargaining requirement before any reduction?
8. What remedy applies: hiring, posting, overtime, back pay, cease-and-desist, or expedited arbitration?
9. Was the clause enforced before, and how did the parties interpret it?
10. Does the clause or an extension expressly survive expiration?
11. If it does not survive contractually, does the underlying staffing practice remain part of the statutory status quo?
12. Could the employer lawfully change it after a genuine impasse, and was the change contained in a pre-impasse proposal?

## If the chair rules the motion out of order

Ask calmly:

1. What exact bylaw, constitutional provision, or meeting rule is the basis?

4. Is an appeal from the ruling permitted, and what is the required procedure?

Do not disrupt the meeting. Preserve the written motion, the ruling, the cited rule, and the minutes for later review.

# 5. Neutral explanation: post-expiration bargaining and strike-decision risks

## A. Expiration does not mean every term disappears

For most private-sector bargaining relationships covered by the NLRA, the employer generally must maintain the status quo on most mandatory terms and conditions while the parties bargain to agreement or a genuine impasse. The expired CBA often helps identify that status quo. The NLRB's public guidance identifies exceptions that can include union-security, management-rights, no-strike/no-lockout, and arbitration provisions.

This means two different questions must be kept separate:

- **Contract question:** Is a clause still enforceable as a contractual promise after expiration or under an extension?
- **Statutory question:** Even if the contract promise expired, must the employer maintain the underlying term or practice while bargaining?

The answer can vary by clause, wording, bargaining history, and the nature of the dispute. Staffing, management rights, grievance arbitration, dues arrangements, and no-strike language need clause-specific legal review.

## B. A verbal deadline is not necessarily a contract extension

"We will keep talking until August 31" could mean a bargaining goal, a temporary no-strike understanding, an agreement to extend all CBA terms, an agreement to extend selected provisions, or no legally binding extension at all. The consequences may differ sharply.

Ask for a signed writing that states:

- start and end dates;
- whether it is retroactive;
- which CBA provisions continue;
- whether either party can terminate early and with what notice;
- whether no-strike/no-lockout and arbitration continue;
- whether proposals or tentative agreements are affected;
- who signed and under what authority.

## C. Bargaining and impasse

The union and employer must bargain in good faith over wages, hours, and other mandatory terms and conditions. The duty requires serious bargaining but does not force either side to agree or make a concession.

After a genuine overall impasse, an employer may in some circumstances implement terms it offered before impasse. Whether impasse exists is fact-intensive. A deadline, a "final offer," a rejected ratification, or a difficult session does not by itself answer the question. The full bargaining history, movement, open issues, mediation, and prospects of further bargaining matter.

Do not assume that the employer may implement a wage or staffing proposal on August 31. Ask counsel to review the bargaining record and any written impasse claim first.

## D. Layoffs and information

Layoff procedures, seniority, recall, safety practices, and effects on employees are commonly mandatory bargaining subjects. Whether the employer must bargain over a particular underlying operational decision can depend on its reasons; even when the decision itself is not a mandatory subject, bargaining over effects may still be required.

The employer's duty to bargain includes providing the union, on request, information that is relevant and necessary to bargain intelligently and effectively. The statutory right ordinarily belongs to the union as bargaining representative, so members should press the bargaining team to send a precise written request and track the response.

If the employer supports a proposal with factual claims about staffing, City scope, revenue, labor cost, vacancies, or service demand, the union should identify each claim and request substantiating information. Confidentiality concerns may require negotiation over safeguards rather than automatic disclosure to every member.

## E. Member rights and limits

Federal law generally protects covered private-sector employees when they act together to improve pay and working conditions, including discussing wages and staffing and circulating a group petition. Protection is not unlimited: knowingly and maliciously false statements, serious misconduct, disclosure of protected security information, and some forms of disloyal public disparagement can lose protection.

For unions covered by the LMRDA, members generally have equal rights to attend and participate in meetings, subject to reasonable rules, and freedom to express views on properly presented union business. Section 104 provides a right to obtain or inspect a CBA that directly affects the employee.

Those rules do **not** automatically create a federal right to receive every bargaining proposal or to vote on every agreement. Under NLRB precedent, employee ratification is generally an internal union matter unless ratification was made a condition of agreement or is required by governing union rules. That is why the Local bylaws, IUOE constitution, bargaining authorization, and statements to the employer must be reviewed.

## F. Outside counsel and the duty of fair representation

The union owes represented employees a duty of fair representation in collective bargaining: it must act fairly, in good faith, and without discrimination. The legal standard allows unions a broad range of judgment; a disappointing proposal, strategic disagreement, negligence, or a decision to bargain without outside counsel does not automatically establish a violation.

Legal consultation can still be prudent here because expiration, extension, staffing, layoff, information, impasse, ratification, City-contract, and prison-safety issues overlap. Members can request counsel without claiming that the absence of counsel itself is unlawful.

## G. Strike decision risks

No individual, informal group, or packet can authorize a strike. Any strike or work stoppage should occur only after the union's lawful authorization process and advice from qualified union-side counsel.

Before any strike vote, members should receive clear answers to these questions:

1. Does any valid no-strike clause or extension remain in effect?
2. Who has constitutional authority to call or approve the strike, and what notice, quorum, and ballot rules apply?
3. What is the lawful objective of the proposed strike, and is it characterized as economic, unfair-labor-practice, or mixed?
4. What notices are legally required, and have they been sent?
5. Could the employer lawfully lock out employees?
6. Could economic strikers be permanently replaced, and what would preferential recall mean in practice?
7. What conduct, intermittent action, partial strike, sit-down action, violence, threat, obstruction, or unsafe shutdown could lose legal protection?
8. What reasonable precautions and orderly turnover are necessary to protect people, secure facilities, and employer property at a prison worksite?
9. What are the effects on health coverage, pension credits, unemployment eligibility, accrued leave, licenses, and immigration or benefit matters?
10. What strike benefits are available, when do they start, and what conditions apply?
11. What is the picket plan, who is trained to lead it, and how will lawful primary activity be kept separate from prohibited secondary pressure?
12. What is the return-to-work and settlement process?

Federal law distinguishes economic strikes from unfair-labor-practice strikes. Both can be protected when lawful, but economic strikers can face permanent replacement and delayed reinstatement; unfair-labor-practice strikers generally have stronger reinstatement rights. Misconduct or an unprotected objective can result in discharge. At a facility involving custody and public safety, abrupt or unsafe cessation of critical work creates special factual and legal risk. Obtain tailored advice before any action.

## H. Deadlines and prompt legal review

An NLRB unfair-labor-practice charge generally must be filed and served within six months of the challenged conduct, subject to limited rules and exceptions. Internal union remedies, grievance deadlines, arbitration deadlines, City-contract notices, and court claims may have different time limits. Preserve dates and consult counsel promptly rather than waiting for August 31.

## I. Issues specifically reserved for a Pennsylvania union-side labor lawyer

- Whether the NLRA covers each worker and whether any guard, supervisor, public-employee, or joint-employer issue exists.
- The legal employer's identity and the effect of the City of Philadelphia contract.
- Whether the CBA expired, renewed, or was validly extended, and which provisions continued.
- Whether staffing language creates a minimum, limit, enforceable remedy, past practice, or post-expiration status quo.
- Whether proposed layoffs require decision bargaining, effects bargaining, information production, or contractual grievance action.
- Whether a genuine impasse exists or implementation would be premature.
- Whether any bargaining conduct supports an unfair-labor-practice charge, grievance, injunction request, or other remedy.
- Whether Local conduct could be arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith; do not infer a breach merely from strategy disagreement or lack of outside counsel.
- What disclosures and ratification procedures the Local 835 bylaws and IUOE constitution require.
- Whether a strike would be authorized and protected, and what safety, notice, replacement, benefit, and discipline risks apply.
- Whether recordings, public statements, or circulation of particular documents are lawful and prudent.
